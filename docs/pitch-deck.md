## Talking points

**02 — The Problem.** Open with the behaviour, not the market: a Filipino fan follows one game across five apps. The league app for the score, two social feeds for the reaction, a betting site for the line, a Viber group for the argument. Then the gap: international outlets skip the PBA, UAAP and PVL entirely, and local sites publish long recaps hours after the moment has passed. Nobody connects the story to the odds fans actually bet on.

**03 — The Market.** Three numbers, no more. 115 million people with basketball as the national conversation; 50 million-plus online and mobile-first; the number one NBA market in Asia. The point is not the size, it is the mobile-first behaviour — this audience reads on a phone in a queue, which is exactly what 90-second stories are built for.

**04 — The Solution.** Three pillars: 90-second stories, odds inside the story instead of on another site, and the SPORTY DAILY email at 7am. Say the design principle out loud: the site should feel like an app, not a newspaper.

**05 — Product & Technology.** This is where the pitch turns from a website into a business. The Editorial Studio takes a story from signal to published in four steps — trend intake, cited research, auto-draft, then grab, edit, publish. The topic bank replaces the blank page. Thirty to fifty wires a day per desk, with auto-post rules the client controls.

**06 — Monetization.** Four pipes: sportsbook affiliates (revenue share or CPA per depositing player), premium ad slots, newsletter sponsorships, and click analytics sold to advertisers as proof of ROI. The line that lands: every pipe is wired into the article, not bolted on.

**07 — Growth Engine.** Explain the 60/30/10 split plainly. Wires chase Google Discover reach and decay in 48 hours. Durable pillars — team hubs, schedules, how-to-watch, betting explainers — rank for months. Most sports sites do only the first and wonder why traffic collapses after a hot week.

**08 — Unit Economics.** Fixed build $15,000–$30,000 over six to eight weeks. Recurring $160–$300 a month with assisted intake, or $80–$200 with editors only. Say what the recurring actually covers: CMS, hosting, email, intake.

**09 — Roadmap.** Four quarters, one job each: launch with sportsbook pilots live on day one; app-feel features and 10,000 subscribers; video shorts in vertical format; brand sponsorships and team tie-ins.

**10 — Next Steps.** The demo is already built and clickable. Six to eight weeks from kickoff to a publishing desk. Ask for the kickoff date, not for approval.

## Objection handling

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "Why not just post on Facebook?" | You rent that audience. The site, the email list and the search rankings are assets you own and can sell. |
| "Is 90 seconds enough?" | It is enough for the score, the stakes and the line. Long-form lives in the pillars, where search intent is. |
| "Won't AI content get us penalised?" | The pipeline requires citations and a human fact pass before publish. The value is speed to a verified draft, not unverified volume. |
| "Why pay recurring if we have editors?" | Recurring covers hosting, CMS, email and intake plumbing. Editors do not maintain infrastructure. |
| "Can we start smaller?" | Yes — launch one league, one pillar cluster, and the newsletter. Add leagues once the capture rate holds above 1%. |

## What not to say

Do not promise traffic numbers, ranking timelines, or affiliate revenue in the pitch. Promise the pipeline, the cadence, and the measurement — those are the things under our control. Everything else is a forecast, and forecasts in a pitch become commitments.
